Let Patrick’s walking rate be \(x\) miles per hour.

Then:

- Tanya’s running rate is \(x+2\) mph.
- Jose’s bicycling rate is \((x+2)+7=x+9\) mph.

Let the distance from school to park be \(D\) miles.

Since Tanya started 1 hour after Patrick, Patrick’s travel time is 1 hour more than Tanya’s:

\[
\frac{D}{x}-\frac{D}{x+2}=1
\]

Since Jose started 2 hours after Patrick, Patrick’s travel time is 2 hours more than Jose’s:

\[
\frac{D}{x}-\frac{D}{x+9}=2
\]

Simplify the first equation:

\[
D\left(\frac{1}{x}-\frac{1}{x+2}\right)=1
\]

\[
D\left(\frac{2}{x(x+2)}\right)=1
\]

\[
D=\frac{x(x+2)}{2}
\]

Simplify the second equation:

\[
D\left(\frac{1}{x}-\frac{1}{x+9}\right)=2
\]

\[
D\left(\frac{9}{x(x+9)}\right)=2
\]

\[
D=\frac{2x(x+9)}{9}
\]

Set the two expressions for \(D\) equal:

\[
\frac{x(x+2)}{2}=\frac{2x(x+9)}{9}
\]

Since \(x>0\), divide by \(x\):

\[
\frac{x+2}{2}=\frac{2(x+9)}{9}
\]

Cross-multiply:

\[
9(x+2)=4(x+9)
\]

\[
9x+18=4x+36
\]

\[
5x=18
\]

\[
x=\frac{18}{5}
\]

Now find \(D\):

\[
D=\frac{x(x+2)}{2}
\]

\[
D=\frac{\frac{18}{5}\left(\frac{18}{5}+2\right)}{2}
\]

\[
D=\frac{\frac{18}{5}\cdot \frac{28}{5}}{2}
\]

\[
D=\frac{504}{25}\cdot \frac12=\frac{252}{25}
\]

Thus the distance is

\[
\frac{m}{n}=\frac{252}{25}
\]

Since \(252\) and \(25\) are relatively prime,

\[
m+n=252+25=277
\]

\[
\boxed{277}
\]
