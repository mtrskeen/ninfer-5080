Let Patrick’s walking rate be \(x\) miles per hour.

Then:

- Tanya’s running rate is \(x+2\) mph.
- Jose’s bicycling rate is \((x+2)+7=x+9\) mph.

Let \(T\) be the number of hours Patrick walked before all three arrived at the park.

Since Tanya left 1 hour after Patrick, she traveled for \(T-1\) hours.  
Since Jose left 1 hour after Tanya, he traveled for \(T-2\) hours.

All three traveled the same distance, so

\[
xT=(x+2)(T-1)=(x+9)(T-2).
\]

First, use Patrick and Tanya:

\[
xT=(x+2)(T-1)
\]

\[
xT=xT-x+2T-2
\]

\[
x=2T-2.
\]

Next, use Patrick and Jose:

\[
xT=(x+9)(T-2)
\]

\[
xT=xT-2x+9T-18
\]

\[
2x=9T-18.
\]

Substitute \(x=2T-2\):

\[
2(2T-2)=9T-18
\]

\[
4T-4=9T-18
\]

\[
14=5T
\]

\[
T=\frac{14}{5}.
\]

Then

\[
x=2\left(\frac{14}{5}\right)-2=\frac{28}{5}-\frac{10}{5}=\frac{18}{5}.
\]

The distance from school to the park is

\[
xT=\frac{18}{5}\cdot \frac{14}{5}=\frac{252}{25}.
\]

Thus \(m=252\) and \(n=25\), so

\[
m+n=252+25=277.
\]

\[
\boxed{277}
