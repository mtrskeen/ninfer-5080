Let \(p\) be Patrick’s walking rate in miles per hour, and let \(T\) be the time in hours after Patrick left when all three arrive at the park.

Then:

- Patrick travels for \(T\) hours.
- Tanya left 1 hour after Patrick, so she travels for \(T-1\) hours.
- Jose left 1 hour after Tanya, so he travels for \(T-2\) hours.

Their rates are:

\[
\text{Patrick: } p
\]

\[
\text{Tanya: } p+2
\]

\[
\text{Jose: } (p+2)+7=p+9
\]

Since all three travel the same distance, we have

\[
pT=(p+2)(T-1)
\]

Expanding:

\[
pT=pT+2T-p-2
\]

\[
0=2T-p-2
\]

\[
p=2T-2
\]

Also, Tanya and Jose traveled the same distance:

\[
(p+2)(T-1)=(p+9)(T-2)
\]

Substitute \(p=2T-2\). Then

\[
p+2=2T
\]

and

\[
p+9=2T+7
\]

So

\[
2T(T-1)=(2T+7)(T-2)
\]

Expand both sides:

\[
2T^2-2T=2T^2+3T-14
\]

\[
-2T=3T-14
\]

\[
5T=14
\]

\[
T=\frac{14}{5}
\]

Now find Patrick’s rate:

\[
p=2T-2=2\cdot \frac{14}{5}-2=\frac{28}{5}-\frac{10}{5}=\frac{18}{5}
\]

Thus the distance from school to the park is

\[
D=pT=\frac{18}{5}\cdot \frac{14}{5}=\frac{252}{25}
\]

So

\[
m=252,\qquad n=25
\]

and since they are relatively prime,

\[
m+n=252+25=277
\]

\[
\boxed{277}
\]
