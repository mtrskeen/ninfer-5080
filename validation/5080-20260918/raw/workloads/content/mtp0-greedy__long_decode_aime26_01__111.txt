Let Patrick’s walking rate be \(p\) miles per hour.

Then:

- Tanya’s running rate is \(p+2\) miles per hour.
- Jose’s bicycling rate is \((p+2)+7=p+9\) miles per hour.

\[
\frac{d}{p}.
\]

\[
\frac{d}{p+2}=\frac{d}{p}-1.
\]

Thus,

\[
\frac{d}{p}-\frac{d}{p+2}=1.
\]

Simplify:

\[
d\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p+2}\right)=1
\]

\[
d\left(\frac{2}{p(p+2)}\right)=1
\]

\[
d=\frac{p(p+2)}{2}.
\]

Jose left 1 hour after Tanya, so 2 hours after Patrick, and also arrived at the same time. Therefore,

\[
\frac{d}{p+9}=\frac{d}{p}-2.

\frac{d}{p}-\frac{d}{p+9}=2.

d\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p+9}\right)=2

d\left(\frac{9}{p(p+9)}\right)=2

\[
d=\frac{2p(p+9)}{9}.
\]

\[
\frac{p(p+2)}{2}=\frac{2p(p+9)}{9}.

Since \(p>0\), divide by \(p\):

\[
\frac{p+2}{2}=\frac{2(p+9)}{9}.

Cross-multiply:

9(p+2)=4(p+9)

\[
9p+18=4p+36

\[
