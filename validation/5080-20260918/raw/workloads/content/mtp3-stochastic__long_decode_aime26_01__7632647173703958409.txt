Let Patrick’s walking rate be \(p\) miles per hour, and let the total distance from school to park be \(D\) miles.

Since Patrick, Tanya, and Jose all arrive at the same time:

- Patrick walks for \(t\) hours.
- Tanya starts 1 hour later, so she runs for \(t-1\) hours.
- Jose starts 1 hour after Tanya, so he bicycles for \(t-2\) hours.

Their rates are:

\[
\text{Patrick}=p
\]
\[
\text{Tanya}=p+2
\]
\[
\text{Jose}=p+2+7=p+9
\]

Because they all travel the same distance \(D\),

\[
D=p t=(p+2)(t-1)=(p+9)(t-2).
\]

First, use Patrick and Tanya:

\[
p t=(p+2)(t-1)
\]

\[
p t=pt-p+2t-2
\]

\[
0=-p+2t-2
\]

\[
p=2t-2.
\]

Now use Patrick and Jose:

\[
p t=(p+9)(t-2)
\]

\[
p t=pt-2p+9t-18
\]

\[
0=-2p+9t-18
\]

\[
2p=9t-18.
\]

Substitute \(p=2t-2\):

\[
2(2t-2)=9t-18
\]

\[
4t-4=9t-18
\]

\[
14=5t
\]

\[
t=\frac{14}{5}.
\]

Then

\[
p=2t-2=2\left(\frac{14}{5}\right)-2=\frac{28}{5}-\frac{10}{5}=\frac{18}{5}.
\]

Thus the distance is

\[
D=pt=\frac{18}{5}\cdot \frac{14}{5}=\frac{252}{25}.
\]

So

\[
m=252,\qquad n=25.
\]

Since \(\gcd(252,25)=1\),

\[
m+n=252+25=277.
\]

\[
\boxed{277}
\]
